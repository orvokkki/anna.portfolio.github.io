    • Designing a Business Matchmaking Tool for Event Attendees

    Helping people meet the right people faster

    Overview

    Timeline
    February – May 2025
    Team
    2 Designers & 4 Developers
    My Role
    UX/UI Designer, responsible for the mobile experience for event visitors
    UpVisit is an event app platform that creates personalised, data-driven event experiences. While it already offered interactive maps and agendas, there was a clear gap when it came to facilitating meaningful networking opportunities for event attendees.
    Impact
    • Designed and launched a functional matchmaking feature within a very short timeframe.
    • Enabled attendees to create networking profiles, discover relevant people, and schedule meetings.
    • Adopted live at several events, with the second seeing 300+ profiles created and 140 scheduled meetings through the app.

    Research & Problem Definition

    Imagine arriving at a busy trade fair with hundreds of people to meet – and no idea where to start. That was the reality for many attendees: too little time, too many possible connections, and no clear way to prioritise (user problem). This often led to missed opportunities, frustration, and lower perceived value of the event. Organisers, meanwhile, wanted to keep visitors engaged and deliver measurable ROI for exhibitors and sponsors (business problem).

    From user persona insights and organiser interviews, we identified these pain points:

    • Attendees wasted time trying to find relevant people, sessions, or exhibitors.
    • Event apps often felt clunky or unreliable, leading to low adoption.
    • Overwhelming choice made it difficult to focus on high-value opportunities.
    • Missed connections reduced the overall perceived value of attending.

    Our target users were B2B event attendees including speakers, sponsors, and exhibitors who needed to make strategic connections efficiently.

    So, we asked ourselves:

    How might we create a seamless matchmaking experience that helps attendees quickly find the right people, focus on what matters, and feel that their time at the event was well spent?

    Competitive Analysis & Project Constraints

    We analysed existing solutions in the market to understand the landscape and identify opportunities for differentiation. Most competitors had spent years developing sophisticated real-time communication features—something we couldn't replicate within our tight timeline so we had to focus on creating immediate value. Besides, the feature had to be designed, tested, and launched in just a couple of months – without a lead designer or PM.

    Despite these, we aimed to deliver a usable, privacy-conscious feature that could launch in time for a real event.

    Implementation & User Feedback

    We didn't have time or budget for formal usability testing, so we tested internally and launched at a real event – Taxtival – to gather live feedback. Early reactions highlighted a few friction points:

    • Some users disliked that profiles had to be completed before accessing the app.
    • Others weren't aware of the feature or missed their invitation.

    Despite this, our next launch at Start-up BW Summit saw much better results: 300+ profiles were created, and over 140 meetings were scheduled through the app.

    Impact & Results

    The matchmaking feature successfully enabled B2B event attendees to:

    • Efficiently connect with relevant participants
    • Schedule meetings in a structured manner
    • Stay organised during complex events
    • Maintain connections post-event

    Whilst the first implementation had challenges, the rapid iteration and real-world feedback led to significant improvements in user engagement and satisfaction.

    Key Learnings

    This project reminded me that no feature is perfect at first release and that real-world testing is invaluable. Even with tight constraints, we managed to design and ship a matchmaking tool that other companies spend years developing – and users actually used it. The key takeaway? Deliver value early, learn fast, and iterate.

    This project taught me several valuable lessons:

    • Perfection isn't the goal on the first try - Creating a functional feature that addresses real user needs is already a significant achievement
    • Real-world testing is invaluable - No amount of internal testing can replace actual user feedback in live environments
    • Constraints spark creativity - Working within tight deadlines and technical limitations led to innovative solutions
    • Privacy by design matters - Building in privacy considerations from the start creates trust and prevents issues later
    • Iteration is everything - The difference between our first and second event launch showed the power of rapid iteration based on user feedback
    This project demonstrated that whilst we couldn't create a feature as sophisticated as what established companies spend years developing, we could still deliver meaningful value to users whilst laying the groundwork for future enhancements. Sometimes, the first step is simply getting started.
